## 1. Company overview

### Business description

Telus Health is the health and wellbeing business within Telus Corporation. It provides digital health, employer wellbeing, benefits administration, retirement administration, virtual care, mental health, pharmacy-related workflow and electronic medical record (EMR) solutions. The business became materially broader after Telus completed the acquisition of LifeWorks in September 2022, adding global employee assistance, benefits administration and workplace wellbeing capabilities. Telus stated that the combined organization served corporate clients in more than 160 countries and more than 50 million lives at close, with approximately $1.6 billion of annual revenue at the time of the announcement. [Telus newsroom, Tier 2](https://www.telus.com/en/health/press-releases/telus-completes-lifeworks-acquisition)

Telus's 2024 annual-report performance materials report **76.2 million healthcare lives worldwide**, up 6.7 million from 2023. [Telus 2024 performance at a glance, Tier 2](https://www.telus.com/about/investor-relations/reports/annual-reports/2024/performance-at-a-glance)

## 4. Industry research and market insights

### Market definition

For Telus Health, the relevant market is not a single generic "digital health" category. It is a multi-segment Healthcare IT and services market.

| Market segment | What it includes | Why it matters for Telus Health |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambulatory EMR and provider workflow | Clinical notes, patient records, practice workflow and provider-facing systems. | Anchors Telus in physician workflows and creates adjacency for ePrescribing, virtual care and AI documentation. |
| Virtual care and hybrid care delivery | Secure text, video, phone-based visits, triage and care navigation. | Supports employer, insurer, provider and public-sector access models. |
| ePrescribing, pharmacy workflow and claims connectivity | Prescription routing, renewals, pharmacy tools, claims adjudication and payment connectivity. | Links clinical workflow to pharmacy, benefits and payer economics. |
| Benefits and retirement administration | Eligibility, enrollment, plan administration, retirement, savings, claims and portals. | Provides recurring employer/plan-sponsor relationships and cross-sell potential. |
| Employee wellbeing and mental health | EFAP, counselling, wellbeing, absence/disability and financial wellbeing services. | Aligns with employer productivity, retention and benefits-cost management priorities. |
| Patient engagement and interoperability | Patient summaries, eReferral, eConsult, data exchange, portals and APIs. | Fits public modernization priorities and may reposition Telus from incumbent EMR vendor to system connector. |

**Market-sizing note:** Tier 1 and Tier 2 sources reviewed did not provide a single consolidated Canadian Healthcare IT CAGR that cleanly maps to Telus Health's mixed EMR, virtual-care, benefits administration and employer-wellbeing perimeter. The report therefore uses authoritative spending, funding and adoption proxies, and flags bottom-up segment sizing as a diligence priority rather than importing lower-confidence market-research estimates.

## 6. Competitive landscape and top players

### Market share and concentration

Comprehensive national market-share data for Canada's full healthcare IT and employer wellbeing market is not available from Tier 1/Tier 2 sources. The most reliable public evidence is for **primary-care EMR concentration**, where the Competition Bureau found that:

- The majority of Canadian health care providers use primary-care EMR systems owned by one of three companies.
- Telus Health, QHR Technologies and WELL Health Technologies are the leading primary-care EMR companies.
- Collectively, these players have as much as 100% share in some provinces.
- The Bureau specifically highlights high switching costs, data lock-in and limited interoperability as barriers to new digital-health competition. [Competition Bureau, Tier 1](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/unlocking-power-health-data)

## 9. Value creation levers and risks

| VCL or Risk | Title | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VCL | Interoperability mandate monetization | Telus can convert pan-Canadian interoperability requirements into integration, patient-summary, eReferral and API services, using its EMR base to become a preferred connector across clinics, payers and governments. |
| VCL | Employer wellbeing cross-sell | LifeWorks gives Telus employer relationships that can absorb mental health, virtual care, absence management, benefits administration and financial wellbeing modules, increasing wallet share if outcomes and utilization are demonstrable. |
| VCL | EMR workflow AI | Physician burnout, search burden and early AI adoption create demand for documentation, search and workflow automation; Telus can embed AI inside existing EMR workflows rather than selling standalone tools. |
| VCL | Benefits administration scale | Benefits and retirement administration create recurring, data-rich workflows where Telus can improve margins through automation, analytics, claims accuracy and bundled wellbeing offerings for plan sponsors. |
| VCL | Pharmacy connectivity expansion | Telus can leverage ePrescribing, pharmacy EMR and claims assets to deepen provider-pharmacist connectivity, supporting medication adherence, renewals, specialty-drug workflows and payer cost-containment programs. |
| VCL | Hybrid care navigation | Telus can integrate virtual care with EMR, employer benefits and mental health triage, helping customers route patients to lower-cost, clinically appropriate channels while maintaining continuity. |
| VCL | Data analytics for payers | If consent and privacy controls are robust, Telus can use claims, wellbeing and clinical workflow data to support risk stratification, absence reduction and targeted interventions for employers and payers. |
| Risk | Anti-blocking margin pressure | Data-access and anti-blocking rules could reduce EMR lock-in, pressure integration fees and make it easier for third-party apps to compete against Telus-owned workflow modules. |
| Risk | Provincial fragmentation drag | Privacy, certification, procurement and reimbursement rules differ by province, increasing implementation costs and slowing national product rollouts despite Telus's scale and brand recognition. |
| Risk | Virtual-care reimbursement uncertainty | Hybrid-care economics depend on permanent payment models, licensing and scope-of-practice rules; weak reimbursement could push growth toward private employer channels and limit public-system penetration. |
| Risk | Employer benefits cost scrutiny | Rising benefits and mental-health utilization may increase plan-sponsor cost pressure, forcing Telus to prove measurable productivity, absence and claims outcomes rather than selling broad wellbeing bundles. |
| Risk | Cyber/privacy trust exposure | Telus Health handles sensitive clinical, claims and employee wellbeing data; a major privacy or cyber incident would damage trust across employers, providers, governments and patients simultaneously. |
| Risk | Clinical AI adoption friction | AI tools may face physician skepticism, liability concerns and uneven data quality, requiring careful governance, explainability and workflow design before Telus can monetize at scale. |
| Risk | Acquisition integration complexity | LifeWorks and historical EMR acquisitions create platform breadth but also product overlap, technical debt and customer-experience inconsistency that could dilute cross-sell and margin upside. |
